# Novel Intake

Intake converts raw enthusiasm into a contract the rest of the book can be
checked against. The author's brainstorm is a pile of sparks: some are load-
bearing, some are guesses, some contradict each other. Your job is to keep every
spark the author clearly cares about, make bold, reversible calls where they
left gaps, and write it all down in a form a later pass can test.

## Read

- The brainstorm/notes in full (they're usually short). Note every explicit
  constraint ("80–90k words", "bittersweet ending", "Station Eleven meets…").
- Project metadata and story structure, in case some of this is already set.
- Any existing docs that look like earlier planning (don't duplicate them).

## Write `Story Bible/Brief & Contract`

Use these sections. Be specific — generic briefs produce generic books.

```markdown
# Brief & Contract — <working title>

## Premise
Two or three sentences: who, what they want, what stands in the way, why now.

## The hook and the question
What makes a reader pick it up; the dramatic question the ending answers.

## Genre, comps, audience
Genre and subgenre; 2–3 comparable titles and *what specifically* we borrow from each.

## Promises to the reader
Numbered. The experiences the book guarantees (P1: a mystery whose solution is fair; P2: …).

## Tone and texture
Emotional register, humor level, darkness ceiling, magic/realism balance.

## Thematic question
Asked, never lectured. How the ending answers it.

## Ending shape
The emotional shape of the ending and the choice that produces it — even if details are open.

## Length and structure target
Target words with tolerance band (e.g. 85,000 ±7%), chapter count, act count, rough words/chapter.

## Drift criteria
Numbered, checkable statements later passes test against the manuscript:
C1. Ines is the only POV character.
C2. Tomas's fate is not confirmed before Chapter 20.
C3. No scene explains the sea's disappearance in scientific terms.

## Decisions made at intake
D1. … (each bold choice you made where the brainstorm was silent, with a one-line why)

## Open questions for the author
Only what is genuinely theirs to decide. Recommend an answer for each.
```

## Also set

- `updateProject` — genre, tone, POV, perspective, voice, themes.
- `updateStoryStructure` — logline (one sentence, protagonist + goal + opposition + stakes).
- Canon facts that intake establishes (ages, dates, names) into `Story Bible/Canon`.
- Ledger → Decisions and Open questions.

## Judgment

- Resolve the brainstorm's open questions where a strong answer exists, and say
  so. "Maybe the town made a deal?" → decide what the deal is, record it as a
  Decision the author can overturn. Leaving everything open stalls every later
  phase.
- Keep the author's words for things they were sure about; paraphrase only the
  maybes.
- Don't outline chapters or draft prose here.
