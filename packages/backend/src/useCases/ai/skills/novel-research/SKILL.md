# Novel Research

Fiction needs plausibility more than footnotes, but a wrong detail an expert
reader catches breaks the spell.

Keep `Story Bible/Research`:

```markdown
| # | Question | Why it matters (scene/chapter) | Current belief | Confidence | Status |
| R1 | How would a maritime insurance investigator document a wreck? | Ines's methods, Ch 2, 5 | photos, hull survey, cargo manifest | medium | open |
```

- Separate what you know confidently from what you'd need to check. Mark
  confidence honestly; never promote a guess into Canon.
- For each open question, note a story-safe fallback (keep it vague, move it
  offstage, or make it a deliberate invention of this world).
- When the author confirms or supplies a fact, move it to Canon with the source.
- Invented-world facts are decisions, not research — record them in World docs.
